How Remote Deck TV handles local discovery, saved settings, TV control and optional integrations.

Effective November 10, 2026

1. How the app works

Remote Deck TV works without an app account. With iOS Local Network permission it discovers advertised media services on your Wi-Fi. When you choose a compatible device it can request authorization and send user-selected commands over that device's supported local protocol. Demo mode is simulated and sends no TV commands. We do not operate a Remote Deck cloud service to store your saved TV list.

2. Information stored locally

Saved device names, local IP addresses, selected platforms, remote favorites and app preferences are stored in on-device app storage. Pairing credentials, client cryptographic identities and optional user-configured Home Assistant tokens are stored through iOS Keychain. These are used for the device features you request, not for advertising profiles.

3. Network access and optional inspection

Remote Deck browses declared Bonjour media services on the Wi-Fi network, and can contact a selected private device IP for identification, pairing or approved remote commands. An optional exact-address inspection may retrieve limited public service information. A separate user-confirmed advanced VIDAA scan may contact up to 254 addresses on the local subnet, potentially including other devices. Only use optional discovery on networks you own or are authorized to inspect. The broader scan is not started in the background automatically.

Your TV, router and Home Assistant hub may log traffic under their own settings and policies. Communication directly between your iPhone and your chosen device does not mean that data is sent to the developer's servers.

4. Optional external services

If you configure a Home Assistant IR integration, Remote Deck sends your chosen command and authentication token to the Home Assistant address you supply. When you open external support links or submit a GitHub issue, the external service may receive standard connection information and the data you choose to provide, under its own privacy policy.

5. Analytics, diagnostics and tracking

The current app is designed without an advertising SDK, third-party analytics SDK, Remote Deck account or cross-app tracking. We do not sell personal information. The optional Copy scan summary action prepares an aggregate, identifier-minimized diagnostic on your iPhone and copies it only when requested. It is not automatically sent to the developer.

6. Your controls and retention

To revoke local-network permission, open iPhone Settings → Privacy & Security → Local Network and switch Remote Deck TV off. To forget an individual saved device, use its Forget TV action in the app. Device-specific saved credentials are cleared where supported, but a shared client identity in iOS Keychain may remain after forgetting a TV or deleting the app. An uninstall is not proof that every Keychain item was erased. Data you explicitly share with an external service is subject to that destination's controls and retention.

7. Support tickets

GitHub issues can display your public account name and anything you post. GitHub processes that information under its own privacy practices. Never submit pairing secrets, private IP addresses, passwords or private device details in public issues.

8. Policy changes and contact

We will update this page before material changes to app data practices. The current policy remains available at this URL. This site is maintained by the independent Remote Deck TV developer (L1gh7C0d35 on GitHub). For support and privacy questions, visit Support.
